# Ethical motivation and engagement

**Kind:** guideline · **Status:** proposed · **Primary layer:** principles · **Owner:** unassigned · **Review:** NOT VERIFIED

## Purpose and boundary

Use encouragement, progress, reminders and reward cues to support goals users endorse, without exploitation, manufactured urgency, dark patterns or overstated outcomes.

**Use when:** A product introduces progress cues, streaks, gamification, reminders or nudges meant to influence repeated behavior.

**Avoid when:** Maximizing attention, conversion or spending at the expense of user autonomy, comprehension, privacy or wellbeing.

**Accountability:** Product/design teams identify user benefit; policy and research specialists review possible harm; app owners enforce actual rewards and status.

## Decision rules and tradeoffs

- Define the goal in terms of users completing a valuable task, not only time-on-app or click growth.
- Use truthful progress denominators, meaningful milestones and forgiving resume behavior; do not punish unavoidable interruptions.
- Offer meaningful control over notification timing, frequency and opt-out; never conceal 'no thanks' behind misleading UI.
- Avoid false scarcity, shame-based streaks, coerced social comparison or a reward that cannot be redeemed as advertised.
- Measure task completion and satisfaction together with pressure, negative outcomes, errors and unexpected repeated use.

## Decision matrix

| Context | Question | Decision support |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Progress | Does it reflect actual completion? | Verifiable numerator, denominator and status |
| Reminder | Does it serve a requested goal? | User controls, opt-out and appropriate cadence |
| Reward | Can people understand eligibility? | Transparent terms and authoritative confirmation |
| Deadline | Is urgency real and relevant? | Accurate dates, no fabricated scarcity |

## Example and anti-example

**Illustrative example:** A learning tool sends optional practice reminders based on user-selected goals and allows pausing without losing accomplishments.

**Anti-example:** A subscription cancellation flow uses guilt, fake countdowns or hidden alternatives to retain users.

## Inclusive operation and verification

Check semantic headings, readable language, focus order, user control and error recovery. Consider long translation strings, RTL, zoom/reflow, reduced motion, multiple input methods, privacy and high contrast as applicable. No screenshot or documentation test substitutes for meaningful user-task evaluation.

| Check | Evidence needed | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Users can explain the true reward and opt-out consequences | Recorded product or study evidence with explicit limitations | NOT VERIFIED |
| Unwanted reminders, manipulation risks and inactivity recovery assessed | Recorded product or study evidence with explicit limitations | NOT VERIFIED |
